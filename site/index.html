Who it helps

Built around Alex.

Our user

Alex Rivera

An older adult with arthritis in both hands. Alex is active and independent: they sign up for library workshops, volunteer at local events and email family to say they got home.

Small taps, typing and picking tiny options are slow and painful. Talking is easy.

The gap

Voice assistants stop at the hard part

They can open an app. Then they leave you at the form, the date picker or the attachment, which is exactly where the tapping starts.

SuuS finishes the job inside the apps you already use: Gmail, Calendar and Chrome.

How it works

From one sentence to a confirmed signup.

  1. 1

    Say it

    "Suus", then the goal in your own words. Say "Stop" any time and it stops at once.

  2. 2

    It reads the screen

    SuuS reads the real screen through Android's accessibility service and plans one step at a time.

  3. 3

    It shows every tap

    A small robot points at each button before tapping it, with a short caption, so you can follow along.

  4. 4

    You say "Submit"

    It reads everything back. Nothing is sent until you say "Submit" or "Send". Then it checks the confirmation.

Under the hood
  • An AI model plans, code does the exact parts. A model on Groq's free tier picks one action per turn from the screen as text. Filling fields, checking the calendar for clashes, verifying the confirmation page and undo are all done by code.
  • Approval is tied to the screen. Your spoken "Submit" is locked to a fingerprint of the exact screen that was read back. If anything changed, it asks again.
  • It only acts where it should. An allowlist of apps, forms only on our test site, and it never submits twice.
  • You choose what it remembers. "What do you remember?" and "Forget …" work by voice and on a big-button screen. Everything stays on the phone.

Setup

A few minutes, once.

The app walks you through it with one large button per step. After that, everything is by voice.

  1. Turn on SuuS in Accessibility settings. This lets it read the screen and tap.
  2. Allow the microphone, so it hears "Suus" and your answers.
  3. Allow the calendar (read only), so it picks times that don't clash.
  4. Allow photos, so it can find "yesterday's dog photo".
  5. Allow notifications.
  6. Tell SuuS about you: the name, email and phone it may fill in. Anything missing, it asks for.
  7. Say "Suus" and the robot pops up listening. You're ready.
