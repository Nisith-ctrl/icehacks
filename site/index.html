The gap

Voice assistants stop at the hard part

They can open an app. Then they leave you at the form, the date picker or the attachment, which is exactly where the tapping starts.

SuuS finishes the job inside the apps you already use: Gmail, Calendar and Chrome.

How it works

From one sentence to a confirmed signup.

  1. 1

    Say it

    "Suus", then the goal in your own words. Say "Stop" any time and it stops at once.

  2. 2

    It reads the screen

    SuuS reads the real screen through Android's accessibility service and plans one step at a time.

  3. 3

    It shows every tap

    A small robot points at each button before tapping it, with a short caption, so you can follow along.

  4. 4

    You say "Submit"

    It reads everything back. Nothing is sent until you say "Submit" or "Send". Then it checks the confirmation.

Under the hood
  • An AI model plans, code does the exact parts. A model on Groq's free tier picks one action per turn from the screen as text. Filling fields, checking the calendar for clashes, verifying the confirmation page and undo are all done by code.
  • Approval is tied to the screen. Your spoken "Submit" is locked to a fingerprint of the exact screen that was read back. If anything changed, it asks again.
  • It only acts where it should. An allowlist of apps, forms only on our test site, and it never submits twice.
  • You choose what it remembers. "What do you remember?" and "Forget …" work by voice and on a big-button screen. Everything stays on the phone.

In depth

How it works, in depth.

SuuS is a Kotlin Android app split into six modules. An AI model decides the next step; plain code does everything that has to be exact.

Every request runs the same loop: look at the screen, pick one action, do it, check it worked. It repeats until the goal is done, it needs to ask you something, or it's time to submit.

  1. voice"Suus, sign me up…"The wake word starts a session. CommandParser turns the sentence into a goal.
  2. deviceRead the screenThe accessibility service captures the screen as a tree of buttons and text. Anything hidden behind another layer is dropped (Occlusion).
  3. agentPick one actionThe goal and the screen, as text, go to openai/gpt-oss-120b on Groq. It answers with exactly one tool call.
  4. overlay + devicePoint, then tapThe robot points at the target. The device layer finds it again on a fresh screen, taps it, and waits for the screen to settle.
  5. agentCheck the resultDid the screen change the way it should? If not, it tries another way.

↺ back to "Read the screen" until one of these happens

  • ask_user SuuS asks you a question out loud and reads the choices.
  • submit Read-back, then it waits for your spoken "Submit" or "Send".
  • done It tells you what it did. "Stop" ends any step at once.

The AI can't press Submit or Send by itself. Those buttons are locked in the device layer, and only open with your spoken approval for the exact screen you heard.

  1. agentRead-backEvery filled field is highlighted and read out.
  2. voiceYou say "Submit"Only the exact word counts. "Yes" doesn't.
  3. deviceFingerprint checkA SHA-256 fingerprint of the screen you approved is compared with the screen now.
  4. deviceTap Submit, onceIf they match it taps, then reads the confirmation page. If anything changed, it asks again.

Selective undo

DraftLedger records each change SuuS makes (text typed, option chosen, photo attached) with what was there before. "Undo the photo" reverses only that change, with no AI call, and checks the rest of the draft is untouched. It refuses if the screen no longer matches what SuuS left.

Other guard rails

  • It only acts inside an allowlist of apps.
  • Forms are only filled on our test site.
  • It never submits the same form twice.
  • "Stop" cancels the current step in about 0.1 s.

Guessing is fine for "what should I tap next?" It isn't fine for typing your email or deciding whether Thursday is free, so those parts are code.

Setup

A few minutes, once.

The app walks you through it with one large button per step. After that, everything is by voice.

  1. Turn on SuuS in Accessibility settings. This lets it read the screen and tap.
  2. Allow the microphone, so it hears "Suus" and your answers.
  3. Allow the calendar (read only), so it picks times that don't clash.
  4. Allow photos, so it can find "yesterday's dog photo".
  5. Allow notifications.
  6. Tell SuuS about you: the name, email and phone it may fill in. Anything missing, it asks for.
  7. Say "Suus" and the robot pops up listening. You're ready.

Android app

Get the app

SuuS 1.0 for Android 11 and newer (tested on Android 15), about 10 MB. No account or API key needed: install it, follow the two-minute setup, and start talking.

  1. Download the APK and open it. If asked, allow installs from your browser.
  2. When you turn SuuS on in Accessibility settings, Android may first ask you to allow restricted settings: SuuS's App info, then ⋮, then Allow restricted settings.

No iPhone version yet. SuuS works by reading and tapping other apps' screens through Android's accessibility service. iPhone doesn't give apps that kind of access, so an iOS version would need a different approach.
